Title: Weather as a Delay Driver — What to Look For
Source: Sample reference doc for AeroFlux Analyst prototype (replace with real NOAA/AWC documentation before production use)

Weather is consistently one of the largest contributors to flight delay across the National
Airspace System. The most operationally relevant conditions include:

- Convective activity (thunderstorms): can trigger reroutes, ground stops, and Ground Delay
  Programs at both origin and destination, and often has cascading effects across a wide
  region rather than a single airport.
- Low ceiling and visibility: forces airports to switch to lower-throughput instrument
  approach procedures, reducing the airport acceptance rate and increasing arrival spacing.
- Strong or gusty surface winds: can restrict runway configuration options (fewer usable
  runways), and crosswind limits may force single-runway operations.
- Winter weather (snow/ice): triggers deicing queues on top of any capacity restriction,
  adding delay even after a runway reopens.

For live serving, METAR observations (routine surface weather reports issued hourly, with
special reports for rapidly changing conditions) are the standard near-real-time source.
For historical model training, the NCEI Integrated Surface Database provides an hourly-quality
archive. Because METAR and NCEI ISD are not field-identical, a feature-engineering layer that
maps both into the same schema is necessary to avoid train/serve skew — a live model should
not see a materially different feature distribution than the one it was trained on.
