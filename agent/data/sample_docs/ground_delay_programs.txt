Title: Ground Delay Programs (GDP) — Operational Overview
Source: Sample reference doc for AeroFlux Analyst prototype (replace with real FAA SWIM/TFMS documentation before production use)

A Ground Delay Program is a traffic management initiative used when the arrival demand at an
airport is expected to exceed its acceptance rate, most commonly because of weather, runway
closures, or equipment outages at the destination. Rather than allowing aircraft to depart and
then absorb delay by holding in the air, the FAA assigns each affected flight a new departure
time, called an Expect Departure Clearance Time (EDCT), before it leaves the ground.

Key characteristics:
- GDPs are scoped to a single destination airport and apply to flights inbound to that airport
  from a defined set of origins.
- Average delay under a GDP is typically communicated as a program parameter and can range
  from under 15 minutes to several hours during major weather events.
- Flights assigned an EDCT that pushes departure into a later slot generally show a
  FlightModify message in SWIM with the updated flight time data.
- GDPs can be revised (extended, compressed, or purged) as conditions change, which produces
  additional FlightModify messages for affected flights.
- Airlines can substitute aircraft/flights within their own allocated slots without FAA
  re-approval, which is one reason flight_ref can change while the underlying GUFI stays
  stable.

Why this matters for delay prediction: a flight departing from an airport with an active GDP,
or arriving at one, is a strong signal of elevated delay probability independent of that
flight's own on-time history, which is why ground-delay-program status is typically included
as a feature in propagation-aware delay models.
